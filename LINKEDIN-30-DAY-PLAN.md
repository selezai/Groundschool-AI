## DAY 3 — Answer Reveal + Explanation

Yesterday's Met answer: **D) 14.4°C**

Here's the working:

ISA lapse rate = 1.98°C per 1,000 ft
From 2,000 ft to 10,000 ft = 8,000 ft
Temperature drop = 8 × 1.98 = 15.84°C
Surface temp 30°C − 15.84°C = 14.16°C ≈ 14.4°C

If you got it wrong — that's fine. That's literally the point of practising. Most students get caught by rounding or forgetting the exact lapse rate.

Every question on Groundschool AI comes with an explanation like this. Not just the letter — the reasoning.

## DAY 5 — Study Tip: Active Recall

The students who pass ground school exams first time almost never do it by re-reading their textbooks.

They test themselves. Over and over.

It's called active recall. Decades of research show it beats highlighting, summarising, and re-reading for long-term retention. Your brain remembers things better when it has to retrieve them — not when it passively sees them again.

Here's what that looks like in practice:

1. Read a chapter of your Met textbook
2. Close it
3. Quiz yourself on what you just read
4. Check what you got wrong
5. Re-read only the parts you missed

Step 3 is where most students stop. They think "I'll remember this" and move on. They don't.

That's why I built the AI quiz feature in Groundschool AI — upload your notes, get a quiz in 30 seconds, and find out what you actually know vs what you think you know.

## DAY 13 — Spaced Repetition

Here's a study pattern that actually works for ground school:

Monday: Take a Met quiz. Score 65%.
Wednesday: Retake Met. Focus on what you got wrong. Score 74%.
Friday: Take a Nav quiz. Score 70%.
Sunday: Mix — Met + Nav together. Score 72%.
Next Monday: Met again. Score 82%.

That's spaced repetition. You're not cramming everything the night before. You're spreading it out so your brain has time to forget a little and then retrieve it again. Each retrieval makes the memory stronger.

Groundschool AI tracks your scores by subject so you can see this progression. It's genuinely satisfying to watch your numbers climb.

The students who pass first time aren't smarter. They just study smarter.

## DAY 14 — Nav Answer + Week 2 Recap

Nav answer: **C) 8° left**

Wind from 030° on a track of 090° means the wind is from ahead-left. Using the 1-in-60 rule: sin(60°) × 20/120 ≈ 8° drift to the right, so you correct 8° left.

If you got this wrong — Nav is the subject where practice makes the biggest difference. The concepts aren't hard, but the calculations need to be fast and accurate under exam pressure.

## DAY 16 — Exam Day Tips

5 things I'd tell every student pilot the night before a ground school exam:

1. **Don't study anything new.** If you don't know it by now, cramming won't help. Review what you already know.

2. **Read every question twice.** Half the "trick questions" aren't tricks — you just missed a word.

3. **Eliminate two answers first.** Most questions have two obviously wrong options. Get rid of them, then think.

4. **Don't change your answer** unless you're certain. First instinct is usually right.

5. **Flag and move on.** Don't spend 5 minutes on one question. Come back to it.

And honestly — if you've been practising consistently, you're more ready than you think. Trust the work you've put in.

Good luck to everyone writing this month.

## DAY 24 — POF Answer

POF answer: **C) 1.41**

Load factor in a level turn = 1 / cos(bank angle)
At 45°: 1 / cos(45°) = 1 / 0.707 = 1.41

This is one of those questions where if you understand the formula, you'll never get it wrong regardless of what bank angle they give you. If you just memorised "45° = 1.41" you're stuck when they ask about 30° or 60°.

cos(30°) = 0.866 → load factor = 1.15
cos(60°) = 0.5 → load factor = 2.0

Learn the concept. The numbers follow.

## DAY 26 — Study Mistake Most Students Make

The biggest study mistake I see student pilots make:

Studying one subject for 3 weeks straight, then moving to the next one.

By the time you get to subject 4, you've forgotten subject 1. Then you go back to revise subject 1 and forget subject 3. It's an endless cycle.

What works better: interleaving.

Study Met for 45 minutes. Then switch to Nav for 45 minutes. Then do a mixed quiz covering both.

Your brain has to work harder to switch contexts — and that extra effort is what builds stronger memories.

It feels less productive in the moment. But on exam day, you'll thank yourself.
