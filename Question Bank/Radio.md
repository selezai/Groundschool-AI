# Radio Communications & Air Traffic Control

## Emergency Signals & Transponder Codes

**What is the 'distress' signal?**
- MAYDAY-MAYDAY-MAYDAY

**What is the 'urgency' signal?**
- PAN-PAN-PAN

**What is a 'distress' condition?**
- A condition of being threatened by serious and/or imminent danger and/or requiring immediate assistance.

**What is the transponder code for an emergency?**
- 7700

**What squawk code is used to indicate radio failure?**
- 7600

**What would you squawk if your radio fails?**
- 7600

**What is the transponder code for unlawful interference?**
- 7500

**If you required assistance, what letter would you lay your signal strips to look like?**
- V

## Radio Communication Procedures

**What is the meaning of the phrase "Acknowledge"?**
- Let me know that you have received and understood this message.

**What is the meaning of the phrase "Go Ahead"?**
- Proceed with your message

**What is the meaning of the phrase "Confirm"?**
- Have I correctly received the following ...? or Did you correctly receive the message?

**What is the meaning of the word AFFIRM?**
- Yes

**What keyword is used when you fix an error in a transmission?**
- Correction

**What quality would a strength 5 transmission be?**
- Perfectly readable

**What quality would a strength 3 transmission be?**
- Readable with difficulty

**If you lose radio communications, how many times must you transmit 'blind' transmissions?**
- Twice

**The term "Confirm" used in radiotelephony is best described as meaning that:**
- I wish to verify the last transmission or instruction from the transmitting party

**Select the correct order of priority (most to least) for the categories of the messages:**
1. Distress messages
2. Urgency messages
3. Direction finding messages
4. Flight safety messages
5. Meteorological messages
6. Flight regularity messages
- Priority: 6,5,4,3,2,1

## Altimeter Settings & Flight Levels

**What does QNH refer to?**
- Height above mean sea level.

**What does QFE refer to?**
- Height above airfield elevation.

**What does QNE refer to?**
- Height above the 1013.2 pressure level.

**What will the altimeter show when QNH is set?**
- Altitude

**What will the altimeter show when QNE is set?**
- Pressure Altitude

**Where is flight level zero?**
- 1013.2hPa pressure level

**Select the correct definition which refers to the standard setting of 1013.2 hpa:**
- The altimeter subscale setting in hectopascals to obtain flight levels

**On what altimeter setting will you fly at or below the transition altitude?**
- QNH

**When should a pilot change the altimeter setting from the local QNH to 1013.25hpa?**
- On the climb passing through the transition altitude for that area

**An aircraft with QNH Set on its altimeter, will have its vertical position indicated as:**
- Altitude

**An aircraft with a standard altimeter setting set on subscale window, will have its vertical position indicated as:**
- Flight Level

**When flying 1 500ft or higher, what will you indicate your intended height with on a flight plan?**
- Flight levels

**In VMC, flights intended to land at a point beyond 25nm from any aerodrome with a published transition altitude must observe a height of:**
- 3000ft as the transition level

## Flight Plans

**How soon must a flight plan be filed, when not in flight?**
- 30 minutes before departure

**How soon must a flight plan be filed, when in flight?**
- 10 minutes before entering controlled or advisory airspace.

**For a flight requiring an ATS Flight plan, at least how long prior to departure must the flight plan be filed:**
- 30 minutes

**How long is a flight plan that has not being activated, valid for?**
- 60 minutes after estimated time of departure

**When is it mandatory to file an ATS Flight Plan:**
- For a flight for which alerting action is required

**As stated in the AIP, the official methods to file an ATS flight plan are via:**
- Telephone, facsimile or internet

**The equipment on board your aircraft is: VHF RT/F, ADF, VOR ILS and mode C transponder. What would you put on the flight plan under equipment?**
- S/C

**Standard equipment "S" as entered in item 10 of an ATS flight plan represents which of the following:**
- VHF Radio, ILS, ADF, and VOR

**When the number of passengers for your flight is not yet known at the time of filing an ATS flight plan, which designator should you place in Item 19:**
- TBN

**When must you notify ATC when your ETA's change?**
- A change of 3 min

**An ATS flight plan needs to be amended on flight when:**
- TAS changes by 5% or more
- ETA changes by 3 mins or more

**According to the AIP, when shall a pilot report position:**
- When flying in controlled advisory airspace or on a flight for which alerting action is being provided

## Search and Rescue (SAR)

**SAR/Normal:**
- Can only be filed for flights where the destination is controlled

**For which airports may SAR/Normal be filed?**
- Controlled Airports

**When can SAR/NORMAL be indicated in item 18 of a flight plan:**
- Only when the destination is a controlled

**When can SAR/NIL be indicated in item 18 of the flight plan:**
- Only when the destination is an uncontrolled

**If Search and Rescue was requested on an ATS Flight plan to an uncontrolled aerodrome, when will the Search and rescue be cancelled:**
- When the pilot notifies ATC

**Alerting services is provided by ATSUs to all aircraft requiring:**
- Search and Rescue (SAR)

**Select the 3 official Search and Rescue phases:**
- Uncertainty, Alert and Distress phase

## Aircraft Interception & Ground Signals

**If intercepted by another aircraft that is rocking its wings and to the right of you, it means:**
- Follow me to a landing terrain

**If intercepted by another aircraft that is rocking its wings and to the left of you, it means:**
- Follow me away from a prohibited area

**A horizontal white or orange T indicates:**
- The take off and landing direction, which is along the bar towards the crossbar.

**A white dumb-bell displayed in a signal area at an airfield means:**
- Aircraft may only use the runways and taxiways

- Remain cleared of controlled airspace and land at an unmanned aerodrome
