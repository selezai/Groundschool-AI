Recalled questions went through the pipeline:
```

```
Some wording gets slightly altered in recall (students don't remember verbatim), but the **structure, phrasing style, and technical terminology** stay intact. These are our gold standard.

### Why Generated Questions Differ

| EASA/SACAA Style | Our Generated Style |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Short, terse stems (1 line) | Longer, more explanatory stems (2+ lines) |
| Clinical, regulatory language | Textbook review language |
| "The term X is defined as..." | "X is defined as the..." |
| Gives the term, asks for definition | Gives the definition, asks for the term |
| "oncoming airflow" | "relative airflow" |
| Ends with `...` or `:-` | Ends with `:-` (good) |
| Options are concise phrases | Options sometimes too verbose |
| Neutral, impersonal tone | Sometimes explanatory tone |

## 2. EASA ECQB Writing Style Rules

These rules are derived from analysis of official EASA ECQB-PPL questions (AustroControl published set) and verified EASA exam recalls.

### 2.1 Question Stem Rules

1. **Keep stems SHORT** — ideally 1 line, maximum 2 lines
2. **End with `:-`** — this is the SACAA/EASA convention (we already do this — keep it)
3. **Use clinical, impersonal language** — no "you", no "a pilot should", just state the scenario
4. **Use present tense** — "The angle of attack is..." not "The angle of attack would be..."
5. **Avoid filler words** — no "basically", "essentially", "in other words"

### 2.2 Common EASA Stem Patterns

Memorize and use these patterns — they appear repeatedly in the ECQB:

```
"The term [X] is defined as..."
"[X] is called:-"
"Which statement is correct regarding [X]?"
"What is the effect of [X] on [Y]?"
"An increase in [X] will result in:-"
"A decrease in [X] leads to:-"
"[X] is the result of:-"
"The purpose of [X] is to:-"
"No person shall [verb] unless:-"
"The minimum [X] required for [Y] is:-"
"[Scenario]. The [value] is:-"
"During [phase of flight], [X] will:-"
```

### 2.3 Option Rules

1. **All 4 options should be similar length** — if one is much longer, it signals the correct answer
2. **Options should be grammatically parallel** — all start the same way
3. **Keep options concise** — short phrases, not full sentences
4. **Avoid "all of the above" / "none of the above"** — EASA never uses these
5. **Distractors must be plausible** — wrong but not absurd
6. **Use consistent formatting** — lowercase start unless proper noun, no trailing periods

### 2.4 Terminology Preferences

Use EASA/ICAO standard terminology:

| Use This | Not This |
|----------|----------|
| relative airflow | oncoming air / wind |
| angle of attack | angle of incidence (unless specifically about wing mounting) |
| aerofoil | airfoil |
| centre of pressure | center of pressure |
| manoeuvring speed | maneuvering speed |
| flight level | FL (spell out in stems, abbreviate in options) |
| hectopascals (hPa) | millibars (mb) — though both appear |
| nautical miles (nm) | NM |
| indicated airspeed (IAS) | can abbreviate in options |
| QNH, QFE, QNE | always uppercase, no periods |

### 2.5 SA-Specific Additions (SACAA Only)

These topics are NOT in the EASA ECQB and are unique to SACAA. Write them in EASA style but with SA content:

- **Air Law**: SA CARs (Part 61, 91, 67), SACAA licensing, SA airspace classification, SA-specific procedures
- **Meteorology**: SA weather patterns (berg winds, coastal lows, ridging highs, line squalls), SA station models
- **Navigation**: SA magnetic variation, SA chart references
- **Radio**: SA callsigns, SA frequency assignments

## 4. CPL Bank Creation Guide

### 4.1 Source Priority (in order)

1. **Real SACAA CPL exam recalls** — highest accuracy
   - Sources: Aeroversity WhatsApp groups, AvCom forum, student networks
   - These should be tagged `"source": "recalled"`
   - Expected wording accuracy: **85-90%**

2. **EASA ECQB ATPL questions (CPL-level topics)** — second best
   - Sources: AustroControl published sets, aviationexam.com free samples, atplquestions.com
   - SACAA CPL draws from the EASA ATPL question bank for shared subjects
   - Rewrite to match SACAA context where needed (SA regulations, SA weather)
   - Expected wording accuracy: **75-85%**

3. **Generated questions (to fill gaps)** — lowest priority
   - MUST follow the EASA style rules in Section 2
   - Tag as `"source": "generated"`
   - Expected wording accuracy: **70-80%** (if style rules followed)

### 4.2 CPL Subjects & EASA Overlap

| CPL Subject | EASA Equivalent | Overlap Level | Notes |
|-------------|----------------|:---:|-------|
| Air Law | ATPL 010 | Medium | SA CARs differ significantly from EASA regs |
| Aircraft General Knowledge | ATPL 021/022 | High | Engines, electrics, instruments — very similar |
| Flight Performance & Planning | ATPL 031/032/033 | High | Mass & balance, performance — nearly identical |
| Human Performance | ATPL 040 | Very High | Almost 1:1 with EASA |
| Meteorology | ATPL 050 | High | Core met is same; SA weather patterns unique |
| Navigation | ATPL 061/062 | High | General nav same; radio nav very similar |
| Principles of Flight | ATPL 081 | Very High | Almost 1:1 with EASA |
| Radio Telephony | N/A | Low | SA-specific procedures and frequencies |

### 4.3 Writing New CPL Questions — Checklist

For every new question, verify:

- [ ] **Stem is 1-2 lines max**
- [ ] **Stem ends with `:-`**
- [ ] **Uses an EASA stem pattern** (see Section 2.2)
- [ ] **Clinical, impersonal language** — no "you" or "a pilot"
- [ ] **4 options, all similar length**
- [ ] **Options are grammatically parallel**
- [ ] **No "all/none of the above"**
- [ ] **Uses EASA/ICAO terminology** (see Section 2.4)
- [ ] **Correct answer is verified** against regulations/textbooks
- [ ] **Explanation references the regulation or principle**
- [ ] **Source is tagged** ("recalled" or "generated")
- [ ] **SA-specific content** is clearly identified in the topic field

## 5. Recall Collection Strategy

### 5.1 Where to Find CPL Recalls

| Source | Access | Quality | Volume |
|--------|--------|---------|--------|
| **Aeroversity WhatsApp groups** | Join via aeroversity.co.za | High — recent, verified | Medium |
| **AvCom forum** | avcom.co.za | Medium — older posts | Low |
| **Student networks** (flight schools) | Personal contacts | High — fresh recalls | Variable |
| **PPRuNe SA forum** | pprune.org | Low — mostly ATPL | Very Low |
| **Reddit r/flying** | reddit.com | Low — mostly FAA | Very Low |

### 5.2 How to Process Recalls

1. **Collect raw recall** — exact wording as student remembers
2. **Cross-reference with EASA ECQB** — find the matching EASA question if it exists
3. **Verify correct answer** — check against CARs/textbooks
4. **Tag as recalled** — `"source": "recalled"`
5. **Note confidence level** — how certain is the student about the exact wording?

### 5.3 Recall Quality Tiers

- **Tier 1 (90%+ accuracy)**: Student wrote it down immediately after the exam, includes all 4 options
- **Tier 2 (75-85% accuracy)**: Student recalls the stem and correct answer, approximate distractors
- **Tier 3 (60-70% accuracy)**: Student recalls the topic and concept, paraphrased wording

### The 100% Barrier

**True 1:1 wording match is not achievable** without direct access to the SACAA/EASA exam database because:
1. The ECQB is confidential — EASA does not publish CPL/ATPL questions
2. SACAA adds SA-specific questions not in the ECQB
3. SACAA updated their database in Nov 2024 — even long-standing recall banks are partially outdated
4. Student recalls are never 100% verbatim

**80-85% is the realistic ceiling** for a well-maintained question bank. This is comparable to what commercial competitors (aviationexam.com, atplquestions.com, Bristol GS) achieve.
